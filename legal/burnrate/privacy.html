Privacy Policy for Burnrate

Last updated: 30 September 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Burnrate ("Burnrate", the "App", "we", "us") handles information when you use the App. Burnrate shows how much of your Claude usage limits you have spent, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and in widgets.

The App is provided by Anton Razguliaev, Individual Entrepreneur ("we"), registered in the Republic of Armenia (state registration number 286.1580989, taxpayer ID (TIN) 20286933).

Short version: Burnrate has no servers of its own and no accounts of its own. Everything it reads comes from your Claude account and your computer, and everything it keeps stays on your devices or in your own private iCloud storage, which we cannot read. We do not sell your data, share it with data brokers, advertise inside the App, or track you across other apps and websites. Nothing is sent to us.

1. Who this policy applies to

This policy applies to the Burnrate apps for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, and to the widgets and Live Activities that come with them.

2. Information we do NOT collect

We do not operate a server, an account system, or an analytics pipeline for this App. Specifically, we do not collect:

  • your name, email address, or any account identifier;
  • the contents of your conversations with Claude;
  • your prompts, Claude's replies, your source code, or file contents;
  • your Claude credentials or session cookies;
  • usage analytics, crash telemetry, or advertising identifiers;
  • your location.

Apart from the purchase records described in section 2a, we receive no data from the App. There is no server of ours for it to send anything to.

2a. Purchases

The App offers one optional, one-time purchase that unlocks additional screens. Payment is handled by Apple through the App Store; we never see your payment details. Apple's handling of the transaction is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

To confirm the purchase and unlock it on your devices, the App uses Adapty (Adapty Tech Inc.), a purchase-management service. Adapty receives the purchase history for this App (the product, the time, and Apple's transaction identifiers) together with a random installation identifier and device identifiers, and technical details such as the app version, device model, system version, language and IP address. It does not receive your name, your email address, anything from your Claude account, or the advertising identifier — its collection is switched off in the App.

We use this only to make the purchase work and to see sales in aggregate. It is not used for advertising and is not used to track you across other companies' apps or websites. Adapty's handling of this data is covered by Adapty's Privacy Policy.

3. Your Claude sign-in

To read your own usage limits, the App signs you in to claude.ai inside a web view, the same way a browser would. You type your credentials into Anthropic's own page; the App does not see them, does not store them, and does not transmit them anywhere.

The resulting session cookie is kept by the system in the App's own website data store on your device. It is used for one purpose only: asking claude.ai for your current usage figures. Signing out of the App, or deleting the App, removes it.

claude.ai is operated by Anthropic, not by us. Your use of it is governed by Anthropic's own terms and privacy policy. We are not affiliated with Anthropic.

4. Information stored on your device

The App keeps on your device: your usage percentages and their reset times, a history of those figures by day, your notification rules, your chosen theme and language, and which commands you have starred in the reference. This data lives in the App's own storage and in the shared container its widgets read.

5. Reading Claude Code logs on Mac

On Mac, the App can count what you have spent by reading the log files Claude Code writes on your own computer, normally under ~/.claude/projects. It reads them only after you pick that folder yourself in a standard macOS panel; macOS does not grant access otherwise.

From those files the App takes token counts, model names, timestamps and the folder name of each project. It does not keep, transmit, or show the text of your conversations. The results stay on that Mac, except for the daily totals described in section 6 — and project and folder names are not among them: they never leave the machine.

6. Sync through your own iCloud account

So that your iPhone and Apple Watch can show figures gathered on your Mac, the App syncs a small snapshot through CloudKit, in the private database of your own iCloud account. Apple stores it; we have no access to it and no ability to read it.

The snapshot contains your usage percentages and reset times, daily totals of tokens and money, a per-model breakdown, and — when you use them — the notification events you configured. It does not contain your conversations, your code, your project names, or your credentials.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, or you turn iCloud Drive off for the App, this sync simply does not happen and the App works with what each device can gather on its own.

7. The telemetry receiver on Mac

If you switch it on in Claude Code, the Mac app can listen for the usage figures Claude Code itself reports through OpenTelemetry. The listener accepts connections on the loopback interface only (127.0.0.1): nothing outside your computer can reach it, and nothing it receives is sent anywhere. This is off unless you configure Claude Code to send to it.

8. Notifications and Live Activities

The App can notify you when a limit is approaching, when a session window resets, and shortly before one ends. Notifications scheduled by time are created on your device and never leave it. Alerts that originate on your Mac travel to your other devices through your own private CloudKit database, as described in section 6.

The Live Activity on your lock screen is updated through Apple Push Notification service. The activity token is a random identifier issued by Apple for that activity; it is shared between your own devices through your private CloudKit database and is used only to update that activity. It does not identify you to us.

9. Widgets and Apple Watch

Widgets and the Apple Watch app read the same snapshot from the shared container on your device. They make no network requests of their own.

10. Links to other services

The App links to claude.ai, to Anthropic's documentation, to the author's website, and to the App Store. Opening them is your choice, and those services have their own privacy policies.

11. Children's privacy

The App is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. Apart from the purchase records described in section 2a, we hold nothing about you; anything else the App keeps is on your own device and under your control. To ask for the purchase records to be deleted, write to the address below.

12. Data retention and deletion

Apart from the purchase records described in section 2a, we retain nothing, because we receive nothing. To remove what the App holds: sign out to drop the claude.ai session; revoke folder access in macOS System Settings to stop the log scan; and delete the App to remove its local storage. To remove the synced snapshot, delete the App's data in Settings → Apple Account → iCloud on your device.

13. Security

All network requests the App makes use HTTPS. Your Claude session is kept by the system in the App's own website data store and is never copied by us. The telemetry listener is bound to loopback only. The App holds no secret of ours on your device.

14. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. When we do, we will revise the "Last updated" date above and post the new version. Significant changes will be reflected in the App or on our website.
