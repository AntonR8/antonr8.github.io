Privacy Policy — Your GF App

Effective date: 4 August 2026

Last updated: 7 September 2026

The short version

Your GF App has no servers of its own, no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. Her name and her cycle dates stay on your phone. We never see them — we could not retrieve them even if we wanted to.

Two things work differently, and both are optional. If your partner uses Ciclia, the two of you can connect the apps, and her cycle dates then reach you through iCloud — hers and yours, not ours. And if you subscribe, that purchase is recorded by our subscription analytics provider: which plan, when. That record carries nothing about her.

You can erase everything at any time from Settings → Erase All Data.

4. What we collect

Subscription events, and nothing else.

The app has no servers of ours, no account system, no crash reporting and no advertising. What you enter — her name, her cycle dates, compliments, saved dates, the tips you marked — never reaches us and never reaches anyone else.

The one exception is your subscription. When you subscribe, renew, cancel or ask for a refund, that event is recorded by Adapty, our subscription analytics provider, so that we can see whether the app pays for itself. What that record contains: which plan, when, in which country, and a device identifier that is not tied to your name or your Apple ID. What it does not contain: anything at all about her — not her name, not a single date, not one symptom.

It is not used to track you across other apps or websites, and nothing is shared with advertisers or data brokers.

Accordingly, our App Store privacy label states "Purchases — not linked to you".

The pair connection. The app can connect to Ciclia, the app your partner may use for her own cycle. The connection is optional, it is off until she invites you and you accept, and it is built on Apple's CloudKit: the data sits in her iCloud account and yours, inside a shared area only the two of you can open. It never passes through any system of ours, and we have no way to read it — a developer can inspect only CloudKit's public database, which this app does not use at all.

What crosses that connection is deliberately short. From her to you: the day her current cycle started, its length, how long her period lasts, and — only if she switches it on in her app — whether she uses hormonal contraception. From whoever paid for a shared subscription to the other person: which subscription it is and when it expires. Nothing else. Not her name, not symptoms, not notes, not anything you have typed on your side.

Dates cross as a day number rather than a moment in time, so that a phone in Los Angeles and a phone in Moscow agree on which day of the cycle it is.

Either of you can end it. She can revoke the connection in her app, and you can disconnect in yours. After that no new data arrives. The copy already on your phone stays until you erase it, because deleting her data off your device is your action, not something her app should reach across and do.

The app asks you to confirm this before you enter anything. Who is responsible for that data. Because the information stays on your device and never reaches us, you — not us — decide what is entered and what happens to it. Under data protection law you are generally the controller of that information, and in most jurisdictions a person recording information about a partner for purely personal purposes falls within the "personal or household activity" exemption. We provide the tool; we do not process the information.

If you are the person being recorded. If someone has entered your information into this app without your agreement, we cannot delete it for you — we do not have it, and we have no way to identify or reach the device it is on. The data can only be removed on that device, through Settings → Erase All Data, or by deleting the app. If you want to raise a concern with us anyway, write to antonr8.apps@outlook.com and we will help however we can.

6. Subscriptions

The app offers optional auto-renewable subscriptions. Payment is handled entirely by Apple. We never see your name, payment card, billing address, or Apple ID.

The app asks Apple whether a valid subscription exists and stores only the answer — a single yes/no value — on your device, so the widget knows whether to show content. That value contains no personal information.

7. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

Everything stays on your device until you remove it. There is no expiry, because there is nothing on our side to expire.

Ending the pair connection stops new data from arriving. The copy already on your phone is removed by Erase All Data or by deleting the app, like everything else.

You can delete everything in two ways:

  1. Settings → Erase All Data inside the app. This permanently removes the name, cycle dates, compliments, saved dates, saved tips, ratings and settings. It cannot be undone.
  2. Delete the app from your iPhone. iOS removes the app's storage with it.

Note that if you use iCloud Backup, Apple may include the app's data in your device backup. That backup is held by Apple under your Apple ID and your control, not ours. You can exclude the app from backups in iOS Settings.

10. Security

Data is held in the app's private storage, protected by iOS app sandboxing and device encryption.

The pair connection. Data in the shared iCloud area is encrypted in transit and at rest. Under Apple's standard protection, Apple holds the keys; it becomes end-to-end encrypted only if the person whose account it is has turned on Advanced Data Protection in iOS. That is each of your own settings to make, and the app neither changes it nor can see it.

Being connected also means that a person with access to her Apple ID could reach what she shares. That is true of anything in an iCloud account, and it is the reason the connection carries as little as it does.

App lock. Because this app holds information about someone else, you can require Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode every time the app opens. Turn it on in Settings → Require Face ID. While the lock is on, the app also hides its contents when it leaves the screen, so the cycle is not visible in the iOS app switcher.

Face ID and Touch ID are handled entirely by iOS. The app never receives your face or fingerprint data — only a yes-or-no answer from the system about whether authentication succeeded.

We recommend turning the app lock on. Without it, anyone who picks up your unlocked phone can open the app and see her cycle.

Reminders and your lock screen. A notification that says what is happening in her cycle is readable by anyone who glances at your phone, and the app lock cannot cover it. That is why Discreet notifications is on by default: alerts show only the app name and a neutral line, and the detail appears after you unlock the app. Turning it off makes reminders more informative and more visible to other people — the trade is yours to make.

Please note that the home-screen widget, if you add one, shows the current cycle phase on your home screen and is visible to anyone who can see your screen. The app lock does not cover it, because widgets are drawn by iOS outside the app. If that matters to you, do not add the widget.

If you do so for purely personal or household purposes, Article 2(2)(c) generally means the GDPR does not impose obligations on you. If you use the app in any other context, it is your responsibility to establish a lawful basis, which for health data means the explicit consent of the person concerned.
